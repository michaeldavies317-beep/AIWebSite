    For parents

    Kids and AI: calm, simple, sorted.

    Your kids are probably already using AI for homework, games or chat. That's OK. A few sensible settings and a couple of good conversations go a long way.

    Get the free checklistSee the guides

    Got a question? Email parents@easystartai.co.uk

    A good first question for your child:

    "Can you show me how you use it? I'd love to try."

    Curiosity first, rules second. Kids open up far more that way.

    What matters at each age

    What to know

    Young children mostly meet AI through voice assistants, smart toys and apps. They may think it's a real friend, so explain simply that it's a clever computer program.

    What to set up

    • Use AI together, on a shared device
    • Turn on child settings for voice assistants
    • Check which apps have built-in chat

    What to know

    This is when chatbots show up for homework. Most popular chatbots set a minimum age, often 13 or older, so check the rules for each one.

    What to set up

    • Agree what "using AI for homework" is OK, and what isn't
    • Set up family controls on phones and tablets
    • Rule of thumb: no names, addresses or photos in chats

    What to know

    Teens use AI like adults do. The focus shifts from controls to good judgement: checking facts, spotting fakes and knowing when not to share.

    What to set up

    • Review privacy settings together
    • Talk about fake images and voices
    • Keep the door open: "You can always ask me"

    The AI safety checklist

    • I know which AI apps my child uses
    • Family controls are on for each device
    • We've agreed what not to share in chats
    • We've talked about homework and honesty
    • Voice assistant settings are checked
    • We know how to report something odd

    Small tips that make AI easier

    Short and practical. Each one has something to try today.

    Everyone1 min

    Say who it's for

    AI pitches its answer at whoever you say. Tell it the audience and the answer gets clearer straight away.

    Try this

    Explain what a pension is to someone who's never had one, in five short sentences.

    Everyone1 min

    Let it ask you questions

    When you're not sure what to ask, let the AI interview you first. The result fits you much better.

    Try this

    I want to plan a weekend in York. Before you suggest anything, ask me five questions.

    Everyone2 min

    Ask for a second go

    The first answer is a draft, not the final word. Ask for it shorter, friendlier or in bullet points. It never minds.

    Try this

    Make that half the length and a bit warmer.

    Everyone1 min

    Check facts that matter

    AI can sound sure and still be wrong. For health, money or legal things, use it to understand the topic, then check a trusted source.

    Try this

    What should I double-check about this answer, and where?

    Business2 min

    Show it an example

    Paste in one email you're happy with, then ask for replies in the same style. It copies your tone far better than a description does.

    Try this

    Here's an email I like: [paste]. Reply to this customer in the same style: [paste].

    Business2 min

    Turn notes into actions

    After a meeting, paste your rough notes in and get a tidy list of who's doing what, by when.

    Try this

    Turn these notes into a table of actions, owners and deadlines: [paste].

    Business1 min

    Keep private data out

    Don't paste in customer names, bank details or anything confidential unless your AI tool is on a business plan you trust. Swap names for "Customer A".

    Try this

    Customer A has asked for a refund on order 1. Draft a polite reply.

    Parents2 min

    Try it together first

    Ask a chatbot something silly with your child. It's the easiest way to start talking about how it works and what not to share.

    Try this

    Write a short poem about our dog who thinks he's a cat.

    Parents2 min

    Homework helper, not homework doer

    Agree that AI can explain and quiz, but the work stays theirs. Most schools are happy with that line.

    Try this

    Explain photosynthesis to a 12-year-old, then give me three quiz questions.
