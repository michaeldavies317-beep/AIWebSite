What's new in AI: December 2026

Your monthly plain-English round-up: what's worth trying, what's changing, and what you can happily ignore. Grab a cuppa, it's a five-minute read.

Worth trying this month

  • Talk instead of type. Most AI apps now have a voice mode (look for the microphone or a sound-wave button). It's brilliant for thinking out loud while you cook or walk. Try asking it to help you plan your week while you make a cup of tea.
  • Show it a photo. Snap a picture of a confusing letter, a plant you can't name or the inside of your fridge, and ask what it makes of it. Remember to cover anything private first.
  • Projects or saved instructions. If you keep explaining the same things, save them once. Our guide Teach AI to write like you walks you through it.

What's changing

  • Apps keep adding features quietly. Menus move and buttons get renamed. If one of our guides doesn't quite match your screen, that's probably why. Email us and we'll update it.
  • AI is turning up inside apps you already use, like email, phones, search and shopping sites. You don't have to use it. It's usually marked with a sparkle icon, and you can often switch it off in Settings.
  • Free versions are generous, but have limits. If you hit a "come back later" message, that's normal. Paying only makes sense if you're using it most days.

Safe to ignore

  • "AI will take over" headlines. Big claims make good headlines. Day to day, AI is a handy helper that still needs you to check its work.
  • Which model is "best" this week. The leaders swap places all the time. For everyday jobs, the one you already know is the right one.

This month's prompt: a calmer Christmas

Help me plan Christmas dinner for [number] people, including [any dietary needs]. Give me a timeline for the day working back from eating at [time], a shopping list grouped by aisle, and three jobs I can do the day before.

Question of the month

"Is it OK to use AI to write Christmas cards and thank-you notes?"

Absolutely, as a starting point. Ask for a few ideas, then add one personal detail only you would know, like a memory from this year. That's what makes it feel like you. Our email templates work for cards too.
