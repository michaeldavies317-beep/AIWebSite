Family set-up guide

Phone, tablet, laptop and console controls, one screen at a time. Do one device a night and you'll be done in a week. Menus move around with updates, so if a step doesn't match, search the device's help pages for the bold words.

1

Start here: a 15-minute plan

  1. List the devices each child uses: phones, tablets, laptops, consoles, smart speakers.
  2. Pick the one they use most and do that first.
  3. Set up a child account rather than letting them use yours. It's what makes the controls work.
  4. Agree the rules together using the family rules poster, so the settings back up what you've agreed.
2

iPhone and iPad (Screen Time)

  1. On your own iPhone, go to Settings, tap your name, then Family, and add your child (or create a child account).
  2. Go to Settings > Screen Time, choose your child, and set Downtime and App Limits.
  3. In Content & Privacy Restrictions, set age ratings for apps, films and websites, and switch on Ask to Buy.
  4. Set a Screen Time passcode that your child doesn't know.
3

Android phones and tablets (Family Link)

  1. Install the free Google Family Link app on your phone.
  2. Follow the steps to create or link your child's Google account and their device.
  3. Set daily limits and bedtime, and turn on app approvals so they ask before downloading.
  4. Under Content restrictions, set Google Play ratings and turn on SafeSearch.
4

Windows laptops and Xbox

  1. Go to family.microsoft.com, sign in, and add your child to your Microsoft family group.
  2. Give your child their own login on the laptop (Settings > Accounts > Family).
  3. In Microsoft Family Safety, set screen time, app and game age limits, and web filters for Microsoft Edge.
  4. For Xbox, install the Xbox Family Settings app to manage play time, purchases and who they can talk to.
5

PlayStation and Nintendo Switch

  • PlayStation: on the console go to Settings > Family and Parental Controls, or use the PlayStation App. Set age levels for games, spending limits, play time and who can message them.
  • Nintendo Switch: install the free Nintendo Switch Parental Controls app on your phone and link it to the console. Set play time, game ratings and restrict chat.

Many games now have AI characters or chat. Restricting chat with strangers is the single most useful setting on any console.

6

AI apps and web browsers

  • Search: turn on SafeSearch in Google (it's on by default for child accounts) and filters in Bing.
  • YouTube: use YouTube Kids for younger children, or a supervised account for older ones.
  • Chatbots: check the age limit first (see the 10 to 13s guide). If your teen uses one, link it to your account where parental controls exist, and go through the privacy settings together.
  • Broadband filters: BT, Sky, Virgin Media and TalkTalk all offer free family filters for every device on your home Wi-Fi. Look for "parental controls" in your broadband account.
7

A check-up every school term

Children grow, apps change, and settings drift. Three times a year, take 20 minutes to:

  • check which apps are installed, and ask about any new ones,
  • loosen limits that no longer fit their age, so the rules still feel fair,
  • re-read the family rules together and change any that need it,
  • update devices, which also updates safety features.

Put the dates in your calendar now: the start of September, January and April work well.
