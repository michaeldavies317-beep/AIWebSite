Spot an AI scam

Scammers use AI too, but the good news is the same simple habits still stop them. Here's what to look for, calmly and without the panic.

What's new

  • Better-written messages. Scam texts and emails no longer have obvious spelling mistakes.
  • Cloned voices. With a short clip from social media, someone can fake a voice on the phone.
  • Fake pictures and videos. Including "celebrities" recommending investments.

What hasn't changed: the four warning signs

  1. Hurry. "Act now", "today only", "your account will be closed".
  2. Secrecy. "Don't tell anyone", "don't call the bank".
  3. Odd payments. Gift cards, crypto, or a "safe account".
  4. Too good to be true. Guaranteed returns, prizes you didn't enter for.

Three habits that beat it

  • Stop and call back. Hang up and ring the person or company on a number you already know, not one they gave you.
  • Agree a family password. A silly word only your family knows. If "your son" calls from a strange number needing money, ask for it.
  • Ask AI for a second opinion. Paste the message (without your details) and ask the question below.

I've received this message: [paste it, with my personal details removed]. What are the warning signs that it might be a scam? What would be a safe way to check whether it's real?

If you're worried you've been caught out: call your bank straight away on the number on the back of your card, and report it to the UK's national fraud reporting service (search "report fraud UK"), or call the police on 101. Forward scam texts to 7726, for free. It happens to careful people too; there's no shame in it.
